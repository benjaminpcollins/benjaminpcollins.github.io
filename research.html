Investigating Sputtering on Lunar Surface Analogues

Bachelor's Thesis Research @ Technische Universität Wien (TU Wien)

I received my first research training during my Bachelor's thesis at TU Wien, where I simulated the surface sputtering of lunar analogue materials under solar wind ion bombardment. This project led to four co-authored publications, one of which appeared in The Planetary Science Journal.

Faced with data collection limitations from commercial proprietary software frameworks, I built and deployed a custom, multi-threaded Python graphical control application interfacing directly with a Tektronix FCA3100 frequency counter. By architecting an active sequential spike-filtering algorithm, this hardware-software integration successfully reduced frequency signal noise to < 3 mHz, yielding a five-fold optimization in real-time structural data resolution.

High-redshift Galaxy Evolution

Master's Thesis Research @ Alma Mater Studiorum - Universitá di Bologna

Thesis: Using JWST to Investigate Dust Energy Balance in Galaxies at Cosmic Noon

The launch of the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) has opened a new window onto the high-redshift Universe, enabling detailed studies of ionised gas and dust emission in distant galaxies. In this thesis, I perform mid-infrared photometry with the JWST Mid-Infrared Instrument (MIRI) for galaxies in the COSMOS field, focusing on the Blue Jay survey (1.7 < z < 3.5).
