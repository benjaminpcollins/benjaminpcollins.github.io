About me

"The universe is under no obligation to make sense to you."

— Neil DeGrasse Tyson

These words have stuck with me ever since I read them in a book that my high-school and former astronomy teacher gifted me for my graduation - Astrophysics for People in a Hurry. Since then, I've come a long way in my pursuit of reality and I can't help but marvel at the profundity of them.

This and other books are just one of the things that inspired me to pursue a career in Astronomy. On this page I tell my personal story.

From Austria to the Cosmos

My story begins in Austria, growing up in a bustling, high-energy household as one of five boys. In a family that big, you quickly learn the value of community, adaptability, and standing out. While my early years were grounded in the beautiful landscapes of Europe, my perspective shifted dramatically during high school.

It started with a fundamental curiosity about the rules governing the physical world. I remember the exact moment in physics class when complex equations stopped looking like abstract math and started looking like a hidden language—a tool code capable of deciphering the universe itself. That realization sparked an intense fascination with space and astronomy. It wasn't just about staring at distant stars; it was the thrilling challenge of understanding the invisible physics behind them. Driven by this motivation, I pursued Technical Physics at TU Wien, a foundation that ultimately launched my journey across Italy and into the global frontier of observational astrophysics in Chile.

The Creative Pulse: Music & Movement

Science is a deeply creative endeavor, and I find that my analytical mind works best when it is balanced by artistic expression. For years, music has been a cornerstone of my life. I grew up playing instruments, learning how to listen, collaborate, and interpret rhythms without words.

Alongside music, I channeled that same creative energy into dance. Engaging with rhythm and movement taught me a unique type of discipline, focus, and improvisational thinking. While coding data pipelines requires structural logic, playing a piece of music or moving on a dance floor requires an emotional presence—a balance that keeps me grounded and completely refreshed when I step back into the lab.

From My Bookshelf

Despite not being the most consistent reader, I do enjoy picking up a good book every now and then to be inspired by. I personally believe that a good book changes the way you think about a certain topic by the time you close it. Here is a curated selection of the books that I have enjoyed reading the most.

Non-Fiction & Science

  1. "The Selfish Gene" – Richard Dawkins

    Don't get discouraed by the provocative title - this book is the ultimate masterclass in science communication. Being a nerd for biology myself, this is hands-down the most enlightening and mindblowing book I have ever read. I recommend this to EVERYONE, no exceptions.

  2. "Thinking, Fast and Slow" – Daniel Kahneman

    A fascinating exploration of the way we think. This book offers various insights into how we make decisions and why our intuition is less trustworthy than we think. Besides being a humbling experience, it is no overstatement to say that this book has changed the way I understand my own mind.

  3. "Guns, Germs, and Steel" – Jared Diamond

    Why did human wealth and power distribute the way it did? Why did Eurasians conquer the Americas, rather than the indigenous peoples conquering Europe? Jared Diamond offers insightful answers to all these questions and many more that you may not have asked yourself until now.

Fiction & Imagination

  1. "Flowers for Algernon" – Daniel Keyes

    I got this book as a gift from a great friend of mine and dear I say she hit the bullseye. This book contains a powerful exploration of intelligence, identity, and ethics and truly captured my attention for multiple days. I didn't leave my house without it and if I could wipe my memory and read it again for the first time, I would.

  2. "The Picture of Dorian Gray" – Oscar Wilde

    A wonderfully introspective story and an all-time classic. I was captured by the story after seeing a play during my time at Loughborough University in the UK. Maybe that's why I love it so much.

  3. "1984" – George Orwell

    My favourite dystopian novel. The vividness and the portrayal of totalitarianism, surveillance, and the manipulation of truth are unmatched. Nothing for people with weak nerves.

Seeking Horizons: Sky & Sea

Astronomy trains you to think in dimensions of billions of light-years, which naturally fosters a deep desire to explore our own planet to its absolute limits. I am someone who thrives on stepping completely outside my comfort zone to experience the world from entirely new perspectives.

This thrill for exploration has taken me from thousands of feet in the air, plummeting on a breathtaking skydiving jump, to the quiet depths of the ocean floor on unforgettable scuba diving expeditions. Floating weightless beneath the waves waves and rushing through the open sky are experiences that remind me how precious and beautiful our tiny corner of the cosmos truly is.
