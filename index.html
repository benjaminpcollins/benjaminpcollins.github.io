Hey, this is Benjamin and welcome to my personal website! I am a first-yearPhD student in astronomy at Universidad de Chile, where I am interested in studying galaxy evolution starting all the way from the epoch of reionisation (EoR). As a member of the Chilean research community, I aim to leverage the unique multi-wavelength capabilities of Chile's world-class observatories to conduct cutting-edge research in extragalactic astronomy. I am also passionate about science communication and outreach, and I am always looking for opportunities to share my love of astronomy with others. Feel free to browse through my website and reach out to me through any of the platforms below!

About

Growing up in Austria, I obtained my undergraduate degree in Technical Physics from the Vienna University of Technology (TU Wien) in 2019. In October 2025, I graduated with honours from the master's programme in Astrophysics and Cosmology from the University of Bologna. As of March 2026, I am a PhD student in astronomy at Universidad de Chile, where I am part of the research group led by Dr. Charlotte Simmonds. My research focuses on topics reaching from constraining dust and ISM properties in galaxies at z \> 2 to critical analyses of SED fitting codes and their underlying assumptions. I am also interested in the epoch of reionisation and the formation of the first galaxies, which I hope to explore in the future using the Atacama Large Millimiter/Submillimeter Array (ALMA) together with the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST). Research

Cosmic noon describes a phase in the history of our universe in which galaxies underwent rapid evolution. This period is further characterised by the peak of the cosmic star formation rate density at z ~ 2. To understand how galaxies involved in this turbulent environment it is necessary to disentangle the different components of a galaxy's spectral energy distribution (SED). Many observations at these redshifts mostly probe the rest-frame optical and UV continuum of the SED, which is strongly attenuated by dust. Many modern SED fitting codes employ dust energy balance to model the infrared emission of galaxies.

Extending the Blue Jay survey with publicly available MIRI observations, I compile a full photometric catalogue for 150 galaxies in the COSMOS field. The resulting data are used to investigate dust energy balance in galaxies at cosmic noon.
